How To Read A Supplement Label

Key Takeaways
  • Serving Size vs. Servings Per Container
  • Named Amounts vs. Proprietary Blends
  • "Other Ingredients"

Supplement labels pack a lot of information into a small space. Knowing how to read one properly makes it much easier to compare products honestly — including GLPro.

Serving Size vs. Servings Per Container

The serving size tells you how much to take at once (1 capsule for GLPro); servings per container tells you how long one bottle actually lasts (30 capsules per bottle). Multiply the two together before assuming a bottle will last a full month.

Named Amounts vs. Proprietary Blends

Some labels list the exact milligram amount next to every ingredient; others group several ingredients into one "blend" with a single combined total. On GLPro's own label, some ingredients are grouped into a combined blend rather than listed individually. Neither approach alone tells you whether a product works, but named amounts make it easier to compare against published research.

"Other Ingredients"

Below the main ingredient panel, most labels list "other ingredients" — things like capsule shells, binders or preservatives. These are not the active ingredients but are worth a glance if you have known sensitivities.

Reading GLPro's Label Specifically

GLPro lists 8 named ingredients. Berberine is included for its role in glucose metabolism support. Chromium is included for its role in insulin function support. Cinnamon Extract is included for its role in glucose utilization support. Alpha-Lipoic Acid is included for its role in antioxidant and glucose support.

Cost Per Serving, Not Just Cost Per Bottle

A bigger bottle is not automatically a better deal. Divide the total price by the number of servings per bottle to get a true cost-per-day figure, then compare that across package sizes before deciding which size of GLPro to order.

Quick Checklist

  • Check the serving size, not just the bottle size.
  • Look for named amounts where possible.
  • Scan "other ingredients" for anything you need to avoid.
  • Compare cost per serving, not just cost per bottle.
